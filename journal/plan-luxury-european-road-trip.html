At a glance

Getting around: Use an open-jaw flight only when one-way fees make sense; read cross-border and insurance terms. The strongest plan gives each important base enough time and keeps arrival days deliberately light.

Places and decisions that shape this trip

Set a humane driving limit

Plan with door-to-door time, not motorway estimates. Two to three hours of moving time is often enough when parking, lunch and scenic stops are added. Alternate transfer days with car-free days.

Check cross-border rules

Ask the rental company to authorise every country in writing. Research vignettes, tolls, low-emission zones, mandatory equipment and one-way fees through official sources shortly before departure.

Select bases before hotels

Choose the right town or valley first, then compare rooms. A beautiful property on the wrong side of a city or pass can create hours of unnecessary driving. Map parking and restricted zones before paying.

Protect the important reservations

Book the first night, limited ferries, special meals and scarce seasonal experiences. Keep ordinary lunches and at least one half-day open. Store confirmations and offline maps on two devices.

Prepare for disruption

Carry realistic insurance, know the roadside-assistance process and avoid non-refundable connections after mountain passes or ferries. A premium itinerary needs a credible bad-weather alternative, not simply a longer list.

Where to stay

Choose hotels for location, parking, arrival ease and service—not merely room category. Compare the exact room, cancellation policy, taxes, parking and meal arrangements rather than relying on a category label. Boutique does not automatically mean quiet, accessible or fully serviced. Request written confirmation for any essential feature, especially air conditioning, step-free access, private outdoor space or electric-vehicle charging.

Transport and access details

Use an open-jaw flight only when one-way fees make sense; read cross-border and insurance terms. Before paying, check the provider’s fuel policy, deposit, excess, permitted countries and roadside assistance. Urban hotels may sit inside restricted traffic zones. For trains and ferries, allow a buffer around flight arrivals and confirm whether reservations are mandatory.

A practical final checklist

  • Confirm passport, driving licence, insurance and cross-border requirements.
  • Save bookings, maps and emergency details offline.
  • Ask accommodation providers about arrival windows and local access.
  • Keep one flexible half-day for weather or an unplanned discovery.
  • Review cancellation terms and pay only through trusted provider channels.
